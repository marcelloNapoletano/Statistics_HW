HMWK 4 · Many systems, many attackers

What happens to the total, the average and the normalised penetration score when the number of systems n grows? Law of Large Numbers and Central Limit Theorem, simulated for the Bernoulli and Rademacher models.

The setting

Imagine m attackers, each one trying to break into the same n systems, one after the other. On every system an attacker gets a result X. In the Bernoulli model X = 1 if the system is penetrated and X = 0 if not (with probability p of success). In the Rademacher model X = +1 or −1 with probability ½ each: a fair, symmetric score with mean 0. After n systems an attacker has a total score Sₙ = X₁ + … + Xₙ. Because every attacker is independent, the m attackers give us m samples, i.e. a distribution of scores.

Sₙ · total score

Its spread grows with n: Var(Sₙ) = n·σ².

Sₙ/n · average score

For Bernoulli it is the fraction of systems penetrated. Its spread shrinks: Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n. Law of Large Numbers: it concentrates on μ (μ = p, or 0 for Rademacher).

Sₙ/√n · normalised score

Its spread stays constant: Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ². Central Limit Theorem: its distribution approaches a normal curve (standard normal N(0,1) for Rademacher, where σ = 1).

Here μ = E[X] is the mean of one step and σ² = Var(X) its variance: μ = p and σ² = p(1−p) for Bernoulli, μ = 0 and σ² = 1 for Rademacher. Use the buttons below to plot each quantity and compare.
